Risk-Driven Management (RDM)

A management framework for making and fulfilling commitments under conditions of uncertainty.

RDM makes Risk the driver of management. Risk evidence is not produced after the decision; it is the justification for the decision.

Designed for people and structured for software. RDM defines a management model that can be applied consistently by managers, supported by software, and increasingly assisted by artificial intelligence.

Why RDM?

In most management frameworks, Risk Management operates as a supporting discipline. Decisions are made, plans are drawn up, work is commissioned, and Risk is then recorded alongside the activity.

Risk-Driven Management starts from a different premise: organisations operate through commitments, and those commitments exist under uncertainty. Management Control is exercised by identifying Threats, assessing their impact as Risks, and implementing Risk Responses that protect the Commitment.

The result is management that is forward-looking rather than historical, evidenced rather than assumed, and accountable rather than merely busy.

The RDM Framework

CommitmentA promise to deliver something specific, at a defined time, in return for something of value.
ThreatAn uncertainty that may affect delivery. Threats exist independently of any specific delivery.
RiskThe forecast impact of a Threat assessed against a specific Commitment.

Technology and AI

RDM is being developed as a computable management framework. Its core entities, relationships, operating rules, registers and state transitions are intended to be explicit enough to support consistent implementation by people, software and AI-assisted tools.

Structured for softwareRDM can be represented through objects such as Commitments, Delivery Contracts, Policies, Internal Constraints, Threats, Risks, Responses, Warnings and Issues.
AI-assisted managementRDM can provide a governed context in which AI assists with TIP, draft Risk generation, TAP preparation, evidence checking, reporting and review.

RDM does not assume that AI replaces management judgement. Instead, it provides a structured governance model within which AI can help identify uncertainty, apply organisational Policies, generate draft Risks, and support managers in making better evidenced decisions.

Learning and practical materials

The Canonical Framework defines the RDM standard. Practical guides, workbooks, templates and future software material are being developed separately to help managers apply the Framework.

  • Quick Start Guide - a short practical introduction to RDM for managers.
  • Implementation Workbook - a worked path for applying RDM to a real commitment.
  • Delivering Manager's Handbook - operational guidance for running RDM day to day.
  • RDM Data Model - the emerging structure for software and reference implementation.
  • Artificial Intelligence and RDM - how RDM can provide a governance model for AI-assisted management.
